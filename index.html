Plunder — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 11 November 2026

This policy explains what information the game Plunder (“the app”), made by the independent developer Reinghord (“we”, “us”), uses when you play, and why. It applies to the app on Android and iOS and to the game server it connects to.

  • No account, no sign-up, no email or phone number needed.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, and nothing is sold or shared for advertising.
  • The only thing you type is a player name, which other players see while you play together.
  • Single player and the tutorial work entirely on your device.

1. Information the app uses

Your player name

You choose a player name (or keep a randomly suggested pirate name). When you play online, the name is sent to our game server and shown to the other players in your game. When you play on a local network, it is sent to the phone hosting the game and shown to the players there. Please don’t use your full real name if you’d rather not share it.

The game server keeps your name only in memory, for as long as the game room you’re in exists. It is not written to a database and is gone once the room closes.

Gameplay

Your moves in a game (cards you play, marks you put on other players, what you tell the table about a goal card) are sent to the game server, or to the host phone in a local network game, so the game can be played. Like your name, this exists only in memory during the game.

Technical connection data

When you open the app, it asks our game server whether online play is available and whether your version of the app is still supported. When you join an online game, the app also sends its version number so the server can check it is compatible.

To connect you to a game, your device’s IP address is necessarily visible to our server. When the server is busy, it uses your IP address for up to about 30 seconds while you wait in the queue for a seat, so that no single address can take all the free seats. It is kept only in memory, is not written to a database, and is not used to identify you. Our server is hosted by Render, which may keep standard access logs (such as IP address and time of request) for a limited time, under its own privacy policy.

App updates

The app checks for small updates through Expo (650 Industries), the service we use to deliver them. When it checks, Expo receives standard technical information such as your IP address, the platform (Android or iOS) and the app version, as described in Expo’s privacy policy.

Saved on your device only

The app stores a few things on your device so they’re remembered next time: your player name, language, colour theme, single-player settings, whether you’ve been offered the tutorial, and a short-lived token that lets you rejoin an online game if your connection drops. This stays on your device and is removed when you uninstall the app or clear its data.

Local network games

If you host or join a game on your local Wi-Fi, the app uses the local network to connect directly to the other phones (on iOS, it asks your permission first). The game code shown to other players is derived from the host phone’s local Wi-Fi address. These games run between the phones on your network; nothing is sent to us. Keeping the screen on during these games is only to stop the connection dropping.

2. What we don’t collect

The app does not collect or access your location, contacts, photos or files, camera, microphone, advertising ID, or browsing activity. It contains no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising tools.

3. Sharing

We don’t sell, rent or share your information for advertising. Your player name and moves are shared only with the players in your game, as described above. Our hosting and update providers (Render and Expo) process technical data only to run those services. The app is distributed through Google Play and the App Store, which have their own privacy policies.

4. Links to other sites

The app has a button that opens our Discord community in your browser or the Discord app. If your version of the app is too old to play online, it can also open Plunder’s page in Google Play or the App Store so you can update. What happens on those sites is covered by their own privacy policies.

5. How long information is kept

  • Player name and gameplay on the game server: only while the game room exists.
  • IP address in the server’s waiting queue: about 30 seconds at most.
  • Data saved on your device: until you uninstall the app or clear its data.
  • Hosting and update-service logs: as long as Render’s and Expo’s policies say, usually a limited time.

6. Security

Online games connect to our server over encrypted connections (HTTPS / secure WebSockets). Local network games travel directly between phones on your own Wi-Fi network and are not encrypted, so only play them on a network you trust.

7. Children

Plunder is not directed at children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your country), and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from them. Because the app needs no account and keeps no profiles, the only information a child could enter is a player name; we recommend using a pirate name rather than a real one. If you believe a child has given us personal information, contact us and we will help.

8. Your choices and rights

You can play under any name you like, play single player and the tutorial with no game data sent to our server (and with no internet connection at all), and delete everything saved on your device by uninstalling the app. Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EU/UK or the CCPA in California), you may have rights to access, correct or delete personal information. Since we don’t keep any after a game ends, there is usually nothing stored to return or delete — but you are welcome to contact us with any request or question.

9. Changes to this policy

If the app starts using information in a new way, we will update this page and its effective date before that change reaches the app.
